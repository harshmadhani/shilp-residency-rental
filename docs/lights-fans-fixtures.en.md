## How to read the ratings (important)

Almost every ceiling fan on Amazon India sits between **3.8 and 4.2**. A 3.8 is not "bad" — for fans it is average, because a large share of one-star reviews are about the regulator, the installation, or a coupler that was never tightened, not the fan.

So ratings only mean something with two caveats:

1. **Sample size.** A 4.1 from **20 ratings** is noise — one angry buyer moves it 0.2. A 3.9 from **30,000** is a fact. Ignore anything under about 500 ratings.
2. **Marketplace pooling.** Amazon and Flipkart merge colour and sub-variant SKUs into one rating pool. That is why the Havells REO shows **3.8 (585)** on one Amazon page and **4.2 (4,842)** on Flipkart — same family, different pool.

**Amazon is the leading indicator here.** Amazon India is stingier with stars than Flipkart — a 4.0+ on Amazon with a few thousand ratings is genuinely hard to get, while Flipkart hands out 4.2s more freely. So the picks below are ranked on **Amazon rating × Amazon volume**, with Flipkart shown only as a second opinion for Flipkart-exclusive listings (starred as *pooled*).

Read the tables as: rating × number of ratings × warranty. Where rating and warranty disagree, warranty wins for a fan you intend to keep.

## Should you buy better fans? Yes — and here is the maths

Fans outlive tenancies. Over 10 years, ₹649 more per fan is **~₹65 a year**. That is cheap for a better motor, a longer warranty, and a lower bill if you ever move back in.

"Better" means five things, not a fancier look:

1. **Warranty length.** Crompton Ariya Briz carries only **1 year** — dropped for that reason alone. Havells gives 2 (door-step on the REO), Atomberg 3.
2. **BLDC motor.** 33–35 W against 50 W. If you occupy later, roughly **₹400 a year per fan** at ₹8/unit.
3. **Proven reliability, not a spec sheet.** Ratings with volume, and a brand with spare parts in the city.
4. **A remote you control.** A remote is fine if you keep a spare; it is not fine if losing it strands the fan.
5. **A look that does not date.** Matte finishes outlast chrome and gold trims, and five matching fans on a white ceiling photograph better than five different ones.

Havells and Crompton have the deepest service coverage in Ahmedabad; Atomberg is thinner. That is the single risk you accept when you buy the better-rated fan.

On ratings, the strongest Amazon evidence in the whole 1200 mm market is the **atomberg Renesa Enzel — 4.1 from 49,952 ratings**. The Ikano (9,488) and REO (585) are far behind, and the Crompton Energion Caelum manages 2.9, which shows the ratings do discriminate.

That matters for your second point: if you might live in this flat yourself, buy the fan you would want to own. The Enzel is ₹3,849 with a 3-year warranty; five of them cost **₹4,995 more** than the REO. If you stay, that is the fan you would have bought anyway.

### Is this overkill?

Two different questions, and they have different answers.

**The budget is not.** ₹1,51,387 is about **five months' rent** at ₹31,000. Spread over a 36-month tenancy that is ₹4,205 a month, roughly **14% of the rent** — normal for an Indian rental, not extravagant. The fans are ₹19,245, or 13% of the fit-out and 0.6 months' rent. Furniture, not fans, is 55% of the spend.

**One part of the spec is overkill:** the **Best tier**. Everything in it — Erica Nuvo at ₹4,549, the ₹4,500 net, Jaquar/Cera bath fittings — buys look, not function. If you want to cut, cut there first; it saves **₹16,635** and costs nothing you would notice as a tenant.

**And one honest correction to my own earlier logic.** The BLDC motor saves roughly ₹1,750 a year across five fans at ₹8 a unit. **The tenant pays that bill, not you.** So BLDC is not a return on your money — it is a listing feature ("low electricity bill, 5-star fans") and a quieter motor. Buy the Enzel for the warranty, the look and the noise, not for payback.

If you did want to trim without touching furniture:

**Value alternative:** Havells Carlo 3 L at ₹3,079 — same 4.2 rating, 2,742 ratings, SS 304 L tank. Saves ₹611 a unit, ₹1,833 for three. If you want to trim, trim here, not on the brand.

**Best:** Havells Instanio Prime 3 L at ₹4,290 direct — 7-year tank, 3-year element, 0.8 MPa, and **two flexible pipes included**, which offsets most of the ₹600 difference at fitting time.

**Installation notes that actually matter for an instant geyser**

- **Mount it close to the shower or basin.** Every extra metre of pipe is hot water lost and pressure dropped — this is the difference between "okay" and "useless" with a 3 L unit.
- **One unit per bathroom.** Never tee one instant geyser to two bathrooms; it cannot keep up.
- **15 A point with MCB, and a 15 A plug top.** The unit ships with a cord and **no plug top** — the electrician supplies the plug top and socket.
- **Don't open the tap full.** A 3 kW element warms water at roughly 2–3 L/min. Fine for a half-open tap and a mug; useless at full flow. Worth telling the tenant once.
- **Hard water is the element killer.** Every 3 L complaint on Amazon is a burnt coil, not a tank. Keep the invoice, and consider the ₹299 two-year extended warranty at checkout — that covers the part the standard warranty expires on.

**Where a storage geyser would have won:** it carries 15 L of pre-heated water, so it survives a full-open tap. You have decided that trade — noted, and it is a real ₹10,800 saving across three bathrooms.

## 15a. Buy best now, or buy cheap and upgrade later?

The test is not "is it nice?" but **"would I rip this out when I move in?"** Anything you would keep, buy properly now. Anything you would happily keep forever at the cheap spec, buy cheap.

| Item | Keep if you move in? | Buy now | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ceiling fans** | **Yes** | **Recommended** (Renesa Enzel), not Best | Expensive to redo (ceiling hooks, wiring), 10+ year life, visible in photos, and a tenant complaint means a callout — but above ₹4,000 a fan buys decoration, not a better fan |
| **Geysers** | **Yes** | **Best** | Plumbed and wired in, and the element is what fails — so buy the unit with the best warranty and service cover, not the cheapest one |
| **Pigeon net** | Yes | **Best** | Fitted to the wall, lasts years, and an un-netted balcony is an instant complaint |
| LED battens | Yes, as-is | Cheap | ₹175 Philips is what you would fit anyway. A "better" batten is ₹250 and a 10-minute swap |
| Bathroom lights | Yes, as-is | Cheap | IP44 Philips is the correct spec at any budget |
| Night lamps | Yes, as-is | Cheap | ₹135 commodity |
| Exhaust fans | Yes, as-is | Mid | Crompton Brisk Air ₹1,350 already has auto shutters; premium adds nothing but ₹250 |
| Curtain rods | Yes, as-is | Cheap | Local SS pipe is permanent. Nobody upgrades a curtain rod |
| Bath accessories | **Yes, as-is** | Cheap | SS 304 local is what you would fit if you lived there. Jaquar buys a logo, not a better shower |
| Mirrors | Yes, as-is | Cheap | 5 mm polished edge is the whole spec |
| Modular kitchen | Yes | **Already best** | IS 710 ply, 1 mm laminate, 2 mm PVC edge, Ebco — this is the keep-forever spec you already negotiated |
| Main doors | Yes | **Already best** | Solid core, ₹5,000 laminate |

**So: two things get the outright "best" treatment — geysers and the net. Fans get the best *value* that still passes the keep-it-forever test, which is the Renesa Enzel at ₹3,849; the fan tier above that is decoration. Everything else is commodity, and buying it cheap now costs you nothing if you later move in, because none of it is what you would change.**

What you *would* actually add on moving in is not on this list at all: cove lighting, a sofa, beds, appliances, and Jaquar-level bathroom fittings. Those stay deferred, and none of them require redoing the work above.

## 17. Spec rules that actually matter

- **Fans: 3-year warranty, BLDC, and a 4.0+ Amazon rating from at least 500 ratings.** A remote is acceptable if you keep a spare one in the flat — the wall switch still works.
- **One model, one colour, all five fans.** Mismatched fans are the fastest way to make a 2,360 sq ft flat look like a PG. Matte finishes age better than chrome or gold.
- **Check the rating *and* the count.** 4.1 from 20 ratings is noise; 3.9 from 30,000 is a fact.
- **Geysers: 3 L instant, 3 kW, SS 304 tank.** Havells Instanio is the benchmark — 5-yr tank, 2-yr element.
- **Geyser points: 15 A with MCB and a 15 A plug top** — the unit ships without one.
- **Pigeon net: 1.5 mm UV nylon, SS 304 anchors, stitched edges.** Not the ₹8/sq ft market net.
- **Bathroom lights: IP44 minimum.**
- **Bath accessories: SS 304, never SS 202.**
- **Battens: 4000K neutral white** for a home.
- **Exhaust: match the cut-out** — 150 mm toilets, 200 mm kitchen.
- **Curtain rods: 25 mm (1"), brackets every 3 ft, 6–8" overhang** each side of the window.

## 18. What not to buy online

- Exhaust fans — fit risk, and the hole must be measured first
- Long curtain pipes — length and bracket quality
- Mirrors — breakage
- Pigeon net — it is measured and fitted on site
- Anything you need **today**

## 19. Order of work

1. Measure the exhaust cut-outs, every window width, and the balcony opening height.
2. Electrician does a **point check**: what exists, what needs a new point, and whether each toilet has a 15 A geyser point.
3. Plumber confirms geyser pressure and inlet positions in all three bathrooms.
4. Get the pigeon net vendor to measure the balcony on the same visit.
5. Visit the Naranpura / SG Highway dealer with these Amazon prices on your phone — fans, battens, exhausts, geysers.
6. Order online only the small items: night lamps, IP44 bath lights, Essco accessories.
7. Buy locally: long curtain pipes, mirrors, SS 304 accessories.
8. Install fans and lights → exhausts → geysers → curtain rods → bathroom fittings.
9. Net the balcony last, after the geysers are in, so the plumber is not cutting net.
10. Keep invoices and warranty cards in the tenant file — the fan and geyser warranties are yours, not the tenant's.
